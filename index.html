🌿 Behaviour Prioritisation Framework

A research tool for identifying high-impact sustainable behaviour change targets. Fixed columns reflect published survey data. Feasibility and Measurability are yours to adjust based on your context.

Each behaviour receives a composite priority score out of 5, computed as a weighted sum of seven criteria. Weights reflect the relative importance of each dimension in selecting suitable intervention targets for sustainability research.

Score = 20/100 × impact + 20/100 × feasibility + 15/100 × measurability + 10/100 × objective + 10/100 × reported + 10/100 × willingness + 15/100 × income

Impact
Environmental impact of adopting this behaviour, rated 1–5. Higher = greater reduction in emissions or resource use.
Feasibility
How achievable the change is for your target group given social, economic, and structural constraints. Adjust with your own data.
Measurability
How well you can track this behaviour in your study context. Adjust with your own data.
Obj. Meas.
Objective measurement availability — whether reliable sensor or administrative data exists to track this without self-report.
Self-rep (%)
Percentage of survey respondents who currently report performing this behaviour.
Self-rep (1–5)
Self-reported adoption score on a 1–5 scale derived from survey data. Higher = more people already doing it.
Willing (%)
Percentage willing to adopt the behaviour if prompted or supported — a proxy for intervention receptivity.
Willing (1–5)
Willingness score on a 1–5 scale. Combines stated and inferred willingness from the source survey.
Diff. Income
Income-group disparity — how much adoption varies across socioeconomic strata. Higher = more inequitable distribution.
Total Score
Weighted composite priority score (see formula above). Updates live when you adjust Feasibility or Measurability sliders.
